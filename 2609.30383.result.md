## The self-keyed seam (the selection face)
The paper's load-bearing claim is that cascading modifications evade per-skill
scanning: the per-skill stealth rate stays >=86% (delta <= 4.7 pts). But the
benchmark population was SELECTED by those same per-skill scanners — the
SkillCascade inner loop iterates until the scanners pass (D.3: four scanners,
Skill-Scanner static+LLM, SkillScan, Skill-Vetter, AIG-Scanner). So the scanner
both certifies (selects the population) and measures the stealth rate. The
falsifier (does the cascade evade the scanner?) reads its witness from the same
address as the selection channel: claim_channel_address =
falsifier_witness_address = scanner_verdict. The acceptance test is itself
self-keyed — a mis-record at the scanner-verdict level (a cascade the scanner
would have flagged but that slipped through selection) slips through both the
claim and the falsifier.

This is the SELECTION face of the self-keyed family (distinct from the
measurement-channel face of 2609.30397, where the GT is the model's own
perturbation response). WITNESS-ADDRESS fires on the undeclared address
identity, not on the read path itself.

## The fair counterweight (why the claim is not vacuous)
The selection-robust evidence is the DELTA (2.5-4.7 pts), which the body
reports (Table 3, per-skill) but the abstract does not foreground. The delta is
measured within the selected population, so it is not inflated by the selection
in the same way the absolute stealth rate is. The honest, non-self-keyed core:
the Behavior-Composition Scanner (Sec 6.4) is a genuinely different instrument
(generative counterfactual, not the per-skill scanner) and catches 30+ pts on
causal cascades -> that is the real, non-self-keyed result, and it is the
paper's strongest.
